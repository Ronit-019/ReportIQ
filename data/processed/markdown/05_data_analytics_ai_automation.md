# **13. AI Safety, Security, Privacy & Responsible Use** 

## **a. AI Security Risks** 

AI systems must consider: 

I. Prompt injection II. Data leakage III. Insecure tool use IV. Excessive permissions V. Model manipulation VI. Training-data risks VII. Malicious input VIII. Output-based attacks

AI applications that retrieve external or enterprise content must assume that retrieved content may contain instructions intended to manipulate the model. 

Systems should separate: 

I. Trusted system instructions II. User instructions III. Retrieved information IV. Tool outputs

AI systems must apply data-classification controls.

# **Appendix K. Enterprise Data & AI Principles** 

Nexora's enterprise data and AI practices are governed by the following principles: 

## **1. Data has an owner.** 

Every important data asset must have someone accountable for its meaning, quality, and appropriate use. 

## **2. Quality is part of the product.** 

A dashboard, model, API, or AI system cannot be considered reliable when the underlying data is unreliable. 

## **3. Access follows purpose.** 

People and systems receive access because they have a legitimate business need, not simply because access is technically convenient. 

## **4. AI is accountable to people.** 

AI can assist with decisions, but accountability remains with the authorized business owner. 

## **5. Automation does not remove responsibility.** 

A workflow executing automatically is still owned by the team that designed and operates it. 

## **6. Security and privacy begin before deployment.** 

Data and AI controls must be considered during design rather than added only after a system is operational. 

## **7. Measure business value.** 

Technical sophistication is not itself a business outcome. 

## **8. Learn continuously.** 

Models, data, workflows, and business environments change. Production systems must therefore be monitored, reviewed, and improved throughout their useful life.
